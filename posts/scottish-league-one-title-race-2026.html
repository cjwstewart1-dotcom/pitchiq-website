SCOTTISH LEAGUE ONE

Who Wins Scottish League One? The Stats Make Their Case

August 2026 · 7 min read · PitchIQ

Scottish League One doesn't get the coverage it deserves. It is a division full of clubs with passionate fanbases, tight grounds and genuine promotion ambitions — and the 2026/27 season looks like one of the most competitive in recent memory. The data points to a fascinating three-way battle at the top.

How League One Titles Are Actually Won

Before picking winners, it helps to understand what separates champions from also-rans at this level. Over the last five League One seasons, the title winner has averaged 2.04 goals per game and conceded just 0.81. That defensive figure is the critical one — no side has won League One in that period conceding more than 1.0 goals per game across the full season.

Goals from set pieces account for 31% of all goals scored in League One — higher than any other level of Scottish football. This isn't a coincidence. At this level, the ability to win aerial duels, deliver consistent corners and defend dead-ball situations effectively is often the difference between a promotion push and a mid-table finish.

The Relegated Club Advantage

As with the Championship, clubs dropping down from the division above carry a significant statistical advantage. League One sides relegated from the Championship have won the title or gained promotion in 71% of cases over the last decade. Their squad depth, fitness levels and coaching infrastructure simply outclass the division's established clubs in the early months.

The key warning sign is fixture congestion. Clubs with cup runs and a small squad regularly see their form collapse between December and February — exactly the period when League One titles are often decided. The club that manages its squad most effectively across the winter stretch almost always features in the promotion shake-up come April.

The Goal Scoring Picture

League One's top scorers in 2025/26 averaged 21.4 goals per season — but the title-winning sides weren't necessarily the ones with the top scorer. They were the ones with the most balanced attacking output: multiple players contributing double-figure goals, rather than one striker carrying the attack.

The average League One title winner scores from 6.2 different goalscorers across the season. Compare that to the average mid-table club at 4.1, and you can see why squad depth and attacking flexibility matters far more than a single prolific centre-forward.

Home Form Is Everything

In League One, home advantage is even more pronounced than at higher levels. Home sides win 52% of matches, draw 24% and lose just 24% — making a strong home fortress the single biggest predictor of a title challenge. The average League One champion loses just 2.1 home games across the full season.

Away form tells the other part of the story. Title-winning sides in League One average 1.71 points per away game — meaning they're picking up at least one point almost every time they travel. The sides that struggle to take anything on the road invariably fall short.

The Verdict

Scottish League One in 2026/27 will be decided by defensive organisation, set-piece efficiency and winter squad management. The club that arrives with the deepest squad, the best dead-ball system and a home record to be proud of by Christmas will almost certainly be lifting the trophy in May.

One thing is certain: it will go to the last few weeks. It always does.
