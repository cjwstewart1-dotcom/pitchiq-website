SCOTTISH CHAMPIONSHIP

Who Wins the Scottish Championship? The Stats Make Their Case

August 2026 · 7 min read · PitchIQ

The Scottish Championship is one of the most unpredictable divisions in British football. Squeezed between the Premiership's resources and the chaos of League One, it regularly throws up title races that defy logic — and the data. But heading into 2026/27 there are some clear statistical signals worth paying attention to.

The Title Favourites on Paper

The clubs relegated from the Premiership last season arrive with Premiership-level squads and Championship budgets — always a dangerous combination. Their xG numbers from the second half of last season, when survival pressure distorted their play, still showed output in the 1.3–1.5 range per game. Drop into the Championship and that firepower becomes a serious advantage.

Historically, relegated Premiership clubs win the Championship at a rate of around 62% in their first season back. The key variable is whether their squad stays intact over the summer. When it does, promotion is almost a formality. When it doesn't, they finish mid-table.

The Home-Grown Challengers

The clubs who finished second and third in the Championship last season are the ones to watch. Their underlying data tells an interesting story. Top Championship clubs averaged 1.18 xG per game last season, but the more telling figure is their xGA — the best-organised defences in the division conceded just 0.94 expected goals per game, giving them a positive differential even against stronger opponents.

Home advantage in the Championship is more pronounced than at any other level of Scottish football. The average home xG in the division last season was 1.31 versus 0.97 away — a gap of 0.34, which is significantly wider than in the Premiership (0.21). For clubs with strong home records, this is a genuine title weapon.

What the Form Table Says

Over the final 10 games of last season — the most predictive stretch for the following campaign — the Championship's top performers were pressing harder and creating more from wide areas than at any point earlier in the year. Teams averaging over 8 wide crosses into the box per 90 in that final stretch historically outperform their xG by 0.12 goals — a small but meaningful edge across a 36-game season.

Set-piece delivery is the other key variable. The Championship's top scorers from set pieces last season accounted for between 28–34% of their total goals from dead-ball situations. Any side that has invested in a specialist delivery system over the summer starts with a structural advantage.

The Dark Horse Stat

Every Championship season produces a club that dramatically outperforms expectations based on pre-season squad ratings. Last year that club won 7 of their final 10 games having been written off by November. The common thread in these overperforming clubs is defensive organisation — specifically, a low rate of shots conceded from central areas (under 4.1 per 90) combined with high-volume pressing in their own half.

Watch the clubs with new managers in 2026/27. Historically, Championship managers in their second full season outperform those in their first by a winning percentage of 8–12 points. Experience of the division matters enormously at this level.

The Verdict

The Scottish Championship title in 2026/27 is most likely to go to a relegated Premiership club with an intact squad, a strong home record and a set-piece system that generates double-figures in dead-ball goals. The division rewards organisation and consistency far more than individual brilliance — which is precisely what makes it one of the most entertaining leagues in Britain to follow.
