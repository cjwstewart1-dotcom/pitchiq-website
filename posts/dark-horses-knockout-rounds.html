KNOCKOUT ANALYSIS

Dark Horses for the Knockout Rounds

June 2026 · 6 min read · PitchIQ

Every tournament has them. The teams nobody quite trusted enough to back before the first ball was kicked, who quietly put together a group stage that has the data pointing at something remarkable. Here are five sides who the numbers suggest could cause serious damage in the knockout rounds — and why the public is undervaluing them.

1. Sweden

Start here, because this is the most data-backed dark horse of the tournament. Sweden's 5-1 win over Tunisia wasn't a fluke result against a weak side — it was a statement of attacking intent backed by an xG profile that reads like a top-four nation's output. They followed it up with a 3-2 win over Senegal, which is a result that barely got discussed outside Scandinavia but deserves wider attention.

Sweden's underlying numbers show a team comfortable in both phases — they press with intensity, transition quickly, and have multiple goal threats rather than one carry-the-team talisman. In tournament football, that squad depth matters enormously over six or seven games. The betting markets have them at long odds. The data disagrees significantly with that pricing.

2. Morocco

Morocco already have the tournament's headline result: eliminating Netherlands on penalties after drawing 1-1. But look beyond the penalty drama and you'll see a team whose defensive structure is genuinely elite. They've conceded minimally, nullified the Dutch attack effectively, and shown the kind of tactical discipline that gives any team — regardless of the opposition — a chance in knockout football.

Morocco's tournament in 2022 proved they can go deep. This squad is stronger, more experienced, and better coached. Their group stage xG-against (the quality of chances they concede) is among the lowest of any team still in the tournament. You win tournaments by not losing, and Morocco are very hard to beat.

3. Mexico

Mexico won Group A without much fuss — 2-0 over South Africa, 1-0 against Korea Republic, 3-0 versus Czech Republic — and precisely nobody is talking about them as genuine contenders. That's exactly the kind of quiet efficiency the data rewards. Their goal difference of +6 across the group stage is among the best of any side, and their defensive record (zero goals conceded in three games) is the kind of foundation tournament winners are built on.

On home soil — the tournament spans the USA, Canada and Mexico — they'll have partisan support at whatever venue they play next. In tight knockout games, that matters. Mexico have a history of going deep in tournaments before the quarterfinal curse strikes. The data suggests this might finally be the year they break it.

4. Norway

Norway scored 4-1 against Iraq and 3-2 against Senegal in the group stage and yet remain almost entirely off the radar in knockout-round discussions. The Senegal result in particular is worth examining: Senegal are not a weak side — they gave France a competitive game — and Norway beat them in a match that swung both ways.

Erling Haaland in international tournament football is a proposition the data struggles to model because there simply isn't enough historical data on him performing at this level over this many games. What we do know is that his club-level xG returns are historically elite. If Norway create chances — and in the group stage they clearly can — Haaland converting them is among the highest-probability outcomes in football. Any tournament needs one team that rides a single world-class performer to glory. Norway could be that team.

5. Ecuador

Ecuador knocked Germany out. Let that sentence sit for a moment. They beat a side who had just scored seven goals in a single group match. That 2-1 win wasn't a smash-and-grab — Ecuador were the better side, controlled phases of the game well, and showed an organised defensive structure that will be difficult to break down in the knockout rounds.

South American sides have a historical advantage in tournament football: they're typically physically robust, comfortable with high-pressure atmospheres and experienced in the kind of cynical defensive play that can frustrate European sides used to open space. Ecuador are not fancied by the markets. They just eliminated Germany. The data says those two facts don't coexist comfortably.

The Common Thread

What unites Sweden, Morocco, Mexico, Norway and Ecuador? None of them are flashy. None of them have the global superstar narrative. What they share is clean defensive records or dramatic proof-of-concept wins against credible opposition — and in tournament football, both of those things are worth more than the pre-tournament reputation of any individual player.

The knockouts reward teams who don't make mistakes. Watch these five sides carefully. At least one of them is going to make a run that surprises everyone — except the data, which saw it coming.
