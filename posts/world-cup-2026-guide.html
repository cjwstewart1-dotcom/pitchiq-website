WORLD CUP 2026

The complete guide to World Cup 2026

June 2026 · 7 min read · PitchIQ

The 2026 FIFA World Cup is the biggest football tournament in history. More teams, more matches, more host nations and more drama than any World Cup that has come before. Here's everything you need to know before a ball is kicked.

Three nations, one tournament

For the first time ever, the World Cup is being hosted jointly by three countries: the United States, Canada and Mexico. The US is hosting the majority of matches with 11 host cities. Mexico and Canada are each contributing three cities, bringing the total to 16 host cities across the continent.

The final will be played at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, just outside New York City — the largest stadium in the tournament with a capacity of over 82,000. Other iconic venues include the Rose Bowl in Los Angeles, the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City and BC Place in Vancouver.

🏟️ The Estadio Azteca will become the first stadium to host a World Cup final match across three different tournaments — having hosted games at Mexico 1970 and Mexico 1986.

48 teams — the new expanded format

The 2026 World Cup is the first to feature 48 teams, up from the 32 that competed in Qatar 2022. This expansion has completely changed the format of the tournament.

Group stage

The 48 teams are divided into 12 groups of four. Each team plays the other three in their group, earning three points for a win, one for a draw and none for a defeat. The top two teams from each group progress automatically to the knockout rounds. The eight best third-placed teams also advance, bringing the total to 32 teams in the Round of 32.

Knockout rounds

From the Round of 32 onwards, it's single-elimination football. Lose and you go home. The tournament then progresses through the Round of 16, Quarter-Finals, Semi-Finals and the Final.

📊 The expanded format means 104 total matches, compared to 64 in Qatar 2022. The group stage alone features 72 games — more than the entire Qatar tournament.

Key dates

  • 11 June 2026 — Opening match
  • 11–26 June — Group stage
  • 1–4 July — Round of 32
  • 6–9 July — Round of 16
  • 11–12 July — Quarter-Finals
  • 15–16 July — Semi-Finals
  • 19 July — Third place play-off and Final

The contenders

Every World Cup comes with familiar names at the top of the betting — and 2026 is no different. Here are the nations that data and history suggest have the best chance of lifting the trophy.

France

The defending runners-up (and 2018 champions) go into 2026 with arguably the deepest squad in international football. The Mbappé generation is at its peak, the defensive structure is solid and France have the tournament experience that matters most when the pressure is highest.

Brazil

The five-time champions have rebuilt around a glittering generation of attackers. Vinícius Júnior is arguably the best player in the world right now, Rodrygo and Raphinha provide genuine width and Paquetá orchestrates from midfield with creativity that few nations can match.

England

After semi-final exits at the last two World Cups and a heartbreaking final defeat at Euro 2020, England go into 2026 with genuine expectation rather than just hope. The squad is experienced, the tactical flexibility has improved and the pressure of past near-misses may finally have been absorbed.

Argentina

The reigning champions have Lionel Messi — possibly at his final World Cup — leading a squad that is well-organised, battle-hardened and tactically astute under Scaloni. They won't be easy to beat.

Spain

The 2010 champions have rebuilt through their youth system and the current generation — led by Pedri, Yamal and Morata — play a brand of football that is both aesthetically brilliant and statistically dominant in terms of xG and chance creation.

Why this World Cup is different

Beyond the expanded format and the three host nations, there are several reasons why 2026 feels genuinely historic:

  • Lionel Messi's farewell — Almost certainly his last World Cup. Whatever happens, we are witnessing the final chapter of the greatest player in history on the world stage.
  • African representation — The expansion to 48 teams has increased Africa's allocation from 5 to 9 teams. Morocco proved in 2022 that African football can compete at the highest level. More opportunities mean more potential shocks.
  • North American football growth — With the USA co-hosting and MLS growing rapidly, football in North America is at an inflection point. A successful tournament on home soil could accelerate that growth dramatically.
  • Data and technology — More than any previous tournament, 2026 will be analysed, dissected and understood through data. Live xG, player tracking, AI analysis — for the first time, fans have access to the same tools as the professionals.

The verdict

World Cup 2026 is shaping up to be the most watched, most talked about and most data-rich football tournament in history. The expanded format means more surprises, more upsets and more drama. The host nations mean more passion and more noise. And the quality of players on show — from Messi to Mbappé, from Vinícius to Bellingham — means the football itself should be spectacular.

Eleven June cannot come soon enough.
