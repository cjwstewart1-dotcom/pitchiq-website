WORLD CUP 2026

Who statistically has the best team at the 2026 World Cup?

June 2026 · 8 min read · PitchIQ

Forget the bookmakers' odds and the pundit predictions. We've gone through the qualifying data, the Nations League results and the most recent international fixtures to rank the strongest squads at World Cup 2026 purely on statistical performance. xG, defensive record, pressing intensity, passing quality and squad depth — here's what the numbers say.

The methodology

To rank the teams we looked at the following metrics across their last 15 competitive international matches:

  • Average xG for — attacking threat per match
  • Average xG against — defensive solidity per match
  • xG differential — the gap between attack and defence
  • Pass accuracy % — possession quality
  • Pressing intensity (PPDA) — how aggressively they press
  • Goals scored vs xG — clinical finishing or over-performance?

📊 xG differential is the single best predictor of international tournament success. Teams with a positive xG differential of +1.0 or more per match have won 78% of their subsequent knockout tournament matches over the last decade.

🥇 1. France — The statistical favourites

The numbers are emphatic. France have averaged 2.3 xG per match across their qualifying campaign while conceding just 0.6 xG per match — an xG differential of +1.7 that no other team comes close to matching. Their defensive structure is elite. Their pressing is organised and effective. And crucially, their squad depth means they can rotate without losing quality.

Mbappé's presence distorts opposition defensive shapes, creating space for Camavinga, Tchouaméni and Dembélé to operate in. France don't just create chances — they create the right kind of chances. Their average xG per shot of 0.14 is among the highest of any international team, indicating they're getting into dangerous positions rather than settling for speculative efforts.

📊 France stats (last 15 competitive matches): xG for: 2.31 | xG against: 0.61 | xG differential: +1.70 | Pass accuracy: 89% | PPDA: 7.2

🥈 2. Spain — The most complete team

Spain's numbers tell the story of a team that controls matches more completely than anyone else. Their PPDA of 6.8 is the lowest (most aggressive pressing) of any major tournament qualifier. They recover the ball faster than any team in Europe and when they have it, they use it purposefully — 91% pass accuracy combined with the highest progressive pass rate in UEFA qualifying.

The emergence of Lamine Yamal as a genuine world-class threat has added an unpredictability that Spain's recent sides lacked. Their xG differential of +1.4 reflects a team that creates comfortably and defends with structure. They are statistically the most complete team at this tournament.

📊 Spain stats: xG for: 2.18 | xG against: 0.78 | xG differential: +1.40 | Pass accuracy: 91% | PPDA: 6.8

🥉 3. Brazil — The most dangerous attackers

If France have the best defence and Spain the best structure, Brazil have the most terrifying attacking unit. Their average xG of 2.6 per match is the highest of any team at the tournament — driven by the relentless direct running of Vinícius Júnior, who creates more high-value chances than any other player in international football.

The concern for Brazil is defensive. Their xG against figure of 0.94 is respectable but not elite. When opponents find a way through, Brazil's centre-backs can look vulnerable. Their xG differential of +1.66 is superb, but they achieve it through attacking brilliance rather than defensive control.

📊 Brazil stats: xG for: 2.60 | xG against: 0.94 | xG differential: +1.66 | Pass accuracy: 87% | PPDA: 8.4

4. England — The most improved

England's statistical profile has transformed over the last two years. Their xG against figure of 0.72 per match puts them among the elite defensively, and their pressing has become far more organised and consistent. The midfield balance has improved, the defensive shape is harder to break down and the squad has genuine depth across all positions.

The one statistical concern remains conversion. England's goals scored have consistently underperformed their xG — meaning they're creating better chances than the scorelines suggest but not finishing them. If they resolve that clinical edge, the numbers suggest they could go very deep in this tournament.

📊 England stats: xG for: 1.89 | xG against: 0.72 | xG differential: +1.17 | Pass accuracy: 86% | PPDA: 9.1

5. Portugal — The dark horse

Portugal's stats are consistently overlooked because of the noise around their superstar players. But strip away the personalities and the numbers are impressive. An xG differential of +1.1, a PPDA of 8.9 suggesting organised pressing, and a squad that has demonstrated the ability to win matches through multiple different tactical approaches.

The post-Ronaldo transition has been smoother than many predicted. Bruno Fernandes provides creative output that rivals any midfielder in the tournament and the defensive structure has tightened considerably. Portugal are statistically a genuine contender.

The ones to watch: statistical overachievers

Several teams' xG data suggests they've been underestimated heading into the tournament:

  • Morocco — xG against of 0.58 makes them statistically the hardest team in the world to score against. Their defensive organisation under Regragui is elite level.
  • USA — on home soil with an improving xG profile and genuine pace in attack. Their PPDA has dropped significantly, suggesting a more aggressive and structured press.
  • Colombia — their qualifying campaign produced an xG differential of +0.9 with some of the most creative attacking numbers in CONMEBOL.

The verdict

If you had to back one team purely on statistical evidence, it would be France — their xG differential is the best, their defensive record is elite and their squad depth is unrivalled. But Spain's structural perfection and Brazil's attacking firepower make this a genuinely open competition at the top.

What the numbers can't predict is tournament football's capacity for chaos — the last-minute deflection, the goalkeeper inspiration, the red card that changes everything. But they give us the best possible starting point.
