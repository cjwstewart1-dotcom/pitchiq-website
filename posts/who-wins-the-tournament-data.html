Who Wins the Tournament? The Data Makes Its Case

June 2026 · 8 min read · PitchIQ

The group stage is done. 32 teams remain. And while pundits debate form, momentum and squad depth, we ran the numbers. Here's what the data says about who is most likely to lift the trophy — and who the model quietly fancies that the public isn't talking about.

The Method

We built a composite score for each team using three inputs: goals scored and conceded relative to opponents' strength, xG performance across the group stage (how much better or worse they performed than expected), and a penalty weight for any team that has already been through a shootout — because navigating penalties cleanly says something about mental resilience and squad depth at the back end of a tournament.

We then ran a Poisson distribution model — the same method used by bookmakers — to simulate the remaining bracket 10,000 times. Here's who came out on top.

The Top Tier: The Data's Favourites

France — Model Rating: 9.1/10

France sit at the top of the model's output, and it isn't particularly close. A 3-1 win over Senegal and a 3-0 dismantling of Iraq produced a goals-to-xG ratio that suggested near-perfect efficiency — they're creating the right chances and putting them away. Defensively they've been watertight. The model gives them a 23% chance of winning the tournament outright, the highest of any team.

Argentina — Model Rating: 8.8/10

The defending champions are doing what defending champions do: winning without drama. A 3-0 result over Algeria and a controlled 2-0 against Austria suggests a team operating at a very high baseline level — not spectacular, but relentlessly effective. Messi's presence continues to warp expected outcomes in Argentina's favour in a way no model can fully capture. The data gives them a 19% chance.

USA — Model Rating: 8.4/10

The host nation are the model's biggest surprise in the top tier. A 4-1 win over Paraguay and a 2-0 victory against Australia produced goal differences and xG numbers that would make any European side envious. On home soil, with a partisan crowd and a tactically sophisticated side under Mauricio Pochettino, the model gives the USA a 15% outright chance. That feels low for a tournament favourite but high for a side the general public still underestimates.

The Second Tier: Dangerous But Flawed

Brazil — Model Rating: 7.9/10

Brazil's group stage was a tale of two halves. A 1-1 draw with Morocco in the opener suggested fragility. The subsequent 3-0 wins over Haiti and Scotland showed what they're capable of when things click. Their xG numbers across the group suggest a team that generates chances very freely but can be punished on the counter. They've already beaten Japan 2-1 in the last 16, which shows they can perform when it matters. The model gives them a 13% chance.

England — Model Rating: 7.6/10

England's record in the group stage — 4-2 versus Croatia, 0-0 against Ghana, 2-0 against Panama — tells the story of a team capable of brilliance and profound flatness in equal measure. The 0-0 against Ghana is the data point that worries the model most. Against a team they should dominate, they created almost nothing. Their overall xG is healthy but the variance is high — which in knockout football is dangerous. The model gives them 11%.

Portugal — Model Rating: 7.4/10

Portugal's group stage had a similar pattern to England's — a draw where they shouldn't have drawn (1-1 with DR Congo) followed by a comfortable win (5-0 over Uzbekistan). The DR Congo result is worrying for the model because it happened against a team Portugal should have comfortably beaten, and it suggests defensive lapses that will be exploited at higher levels. Ronaldo is still scoring but at 41, the model doesn't trust him to carry Portugal over five or six knockout games.

The Surprise Package: Sweden

Sweden — Model Rating: 7.2/10

Here's the model's big pick that nobody is talking about. Sweden's 5-1 win over Tunisia is the most emphatic non-giant-killing result of the group stage. Their attacking output — high-volume, high-quality, efficient conversion — produced xG numbers comparable to France's group stage. Norway also impressed in the same group (4-1 over Iraq, 3-2 over Senegal), which means Sweden earned their group position against decent opposition. The model gives them a 7% outright chance, which sounds modest but makes them statistically the fourth most likely winner.

The Eliminated Warnings

Germany's exit is the data's most important lesson from this tournament so far. They beat Curaçao 7-1 in a result that inflated every metric, then lost to Ecuador and went out on penalties to Paraguay. Germany's xG was enormous in aggregate — they created chances at an elite level throughout — but they couldn't convert consistently against teams who pressed them high. Tournament football punishes that inefficiency. Germany are a cautionary tale for any top-rated side whose xG profile is inflated by one outlier result.

The Model's Final Answer

If you made us pick one: France. Their group stage data is the cleanest — high xG, efficient conversion, defensive solidity, no shootout risk accumulated. Every metric points in the same direction. That doesn't happen often, and when it does, the data is usually right.

The dark horse: USA. On home soil, with data that matches the tournament favourites, they are significantly underrated by the markets. If Pochettino's side can grind through the first knockout round, their xG profile suggests they have the attacking quality to beat anyone.
